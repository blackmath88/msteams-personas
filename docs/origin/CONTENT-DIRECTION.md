# Content Direction — Two-Sided Working Worlds

## Core model

Every working world has two sides.

### Side A — Their working world

Describe the team or person **without starting from Microsoft 365**.

Show:

- who they are;
- what they do;
- who they work with;
- recurring rhythm / lifecycle;
- information and artefacts;
- technologies used today;
- what is personal;
- what is shared;
- what is institutional;
- concrete challenges.

### Side B — A good M365 working model

Translate the same work into a sensible M365-supported model.

Show:

- what should remain as-is;
- what should move;
- what becomes shared;
- what stays personal;
- what stays outside M365;
- recommended communication pattern;
- document model;
- task model;
- knowledge model;
- attention / meeting model;
- anti-patterns;
- key trade-offs.

Do not present Side B as a universal target architecture.

It is a contextual recommendation.

---

## Current technology vocabulary

The fictional worlds may use combinations of:

- Outlook / email;
- Outlook calendar;
- Confluence;
- university intranet;
- ITS-managed shared cloud storage / shared drive;
- local device storage;
- OneDrive;
- Word / Excel / PowerPoint;
- Zoom;
- Teams meetings;
- personal notes;
- specialist research systems;
- GitHub / code repositories where relevant.

Not every technology is a problem.

The UI should distinguish:

- tool that still has a valid role;
- tool being used for the wrong job;
- duplicate source of truth;
- personal tool that became an organisational dependency;
- specialist system that should remain outside M365.

## Side-B translation categories

Use these categories consistently across worlds.

### Communication

Questions:
- who needs to see this?
- how long should it stay useful?
- is it team knowledge or temporary coordination?
- is the audience internal or external?

Possible M365 translation:
- channel posts for durable team context;
- chat for temporary/direct coordination;
- email for formal / external / boundary-crossing communication where appropriate;
- calls for genuinely synchronous needs.

### Documents

Questions:
- who owns it?
- who edits it?
- should it survive one person's departure?
- is it a personal draft, shared work, or institutional record?

Possible translation:
- OneDrive for personal working material;
- Teams / SharePoint for shared team work;
- links rather than copies;
- specialist repositories for data that does not belong in M365.

### Tasks

Questions:
- does anyone else need to know the status?
- is there one accountable owner?
- is this a personal reminder or a shared commitment?
- does the team have enough task volume to justify Planner?

Possible translation:
- To Do for personal task organisation;
- Planner for shared commitments;
- simple lists / meeting actions where Planner would be overkill.

### Knowledge

Questions:
- is this evolving working knowledge or stable reference information?
- who maintains it?
- who needs it later?

Possible translation:
- Loop / OneNote for evolving collaborative working content where appropriate;
- SharePoint / institutional systems for durable reference;
- intranet remains appropriate for authoritative organisational information.

### Meetings / calendar / attention

Questions:
- what requires a meeting?
- what can be asynchronous?
- what deserves a notification?
- how should working hours and availability be respected?

Possible translation:
- Teams meetings / Outlook calendar;
- activity and notification settings;
- working hours / focus;
- team conventions.

## World-specific content emphasis

### Campus & People Development

Side A should emphasise:

- stable five-person team;
- close work with another internal team;
- recurring services;
- temporary cross-unit projects;
- occasional externals;
- email, calendar, shared drive, intranet, Excel tracking;
- Leadership Forum as concrete scenario.

Main tension:

> permanent organisational structure vs temporary project structure.

Side B should answer:

- existing team vs project team;
- channels by durable work context;
- project channel vs separate workspace;
- shared documents;
- Planner where status visibility matters;
- external access only where needed.

---

### Urban Health Analytics

Side A should emphasise:

- research group;
- shared infrastructure and administration;
- shared funded projects;
- highly individual research work;
- lab/equipment / sensor context;
- specialist research data systems;
- personal drafts and analyses.

Main tension:

> what stays personal, what becomes shared, and what must stay outside M365.

Side B should explicitly show three zones:

1. **Personal**
2. **Shared M365 collaboration**
3. **Specialist / governed systems outside M365**

Avoid implying scientific datasets or sensitive research material should simply move into Teams.

---

### Dr. Nora Keller

Side A should emphasise:

- mostly individual work;
- research + teaching + supervision;
- selective collaboration;
- personal files and task organisation;
- occasional workshops / coauthoring.

Main tension:

> M365 should support collaboration without forcing all work to become collaborative.

Side B should be intentionally lightweight:

- personal drafts in OneDrive;
- personal tasks in To Do;
- join existing shared contexts when collaboration becomes real;
- move a document into a shared context only when shared ownership emerges;
- no new Team just to give Nora somewhere to store files.

---

### Kommission Digitale Zusammenarbeit

Side A should emphasise:

- six meetings per year;
- changing membership;
- secretariat continuity;
- agendas, papers, minutes, decisions, actions;
- low daily collaboration.

Main tension:

> continuity and findability without overengineering.

Side B should be minimal:

- simple shared workspace if justified;
- one clear document structure;
- durable decisions / minutes;
- lightweight action tracking;
- perhaps no Planner;
- perhaps no separate team if an existing institutional space already solves the need.

This world should visibly challenge "more Teams = better".

---

### Teaching world

Side A should emphasise:

- semester rhythm;
- repeatability;
- lecturer(s) + teaching assistant;
- students at the edge, not as ordinary staff-team members;
- preparation, teaching delivery, handover, archiving.

Main tension:

> recurring lifecycle and handover across semesters.

Side B should show:

- what belongs in the staff working context;
- what belongs in the LMS / student-facing environment;
- where templates and reusable materials live;
- personal preparation vs shared teaching assets.

Do not turn Teams into a replacement for the learning-management system.

---

## Content tone

Use short, concrete statements.

Good:

> The team has three places for the same deadline.

> Amira's paper begins as her work. It stops being only her work when co-authoring becomes real.

> The commission meets six times a year. It does not need a miniature project office.

Avoid:

> The team experiences fragmented information architecture across heterogeneous collaboration modalities.

---

## Required content per world

Each world needs:

- one-sentence identity;
- 3–7 people / roles;
- team rhythm / lifecycle;
- 4–8 recurring work activities;
- current technology landscape;
- 4–8 artefacts;
- 3–6 characteristic challenges;
- one primary scenario;
- personal/shared/institutional boundary;
- M365 translation by category;
- 2–4 explicit recommendations;
- 1–3 "keep as-is / keep outside M365" statements;
- 1–3 anti-patterns;
- optional training hooks.

Training hooks are secondary content and should not shape the primary UI.
